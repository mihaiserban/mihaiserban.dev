Information, not medical advice

Labelura gives general information about food ingredients. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition, and it does not replace your doctor, allergist or dietitian. "Not found on this label" is never a promise that a product is free of something. It means only that it was not read in the ingredients we recognised. Labels can be misread, recipes change, and a database record can be out of date. If you have a serious allergy, always read the pack itself before you eat, and follow your clinician's advice.

Results and their limits

  • Results come from a fixed rule table with a cited source for every flag. Sources can change, and new evidence can appear after our last review.
  • Text recognition can misread a label. Check the ingredient list the app shows against the product itself.
  • The app only checks the ingredients it recognises, and it lists the words it could not recognise. When it could read only part of a label, it says so.
  • Diet presets such as vegan, halal or kosher check the ingredient list only. They are not a certification, and they cannot tell how an ingredient was made or handled.
  • The processing estimate and the nutrition levels are estimates from what the label says. They are not a rating of the product.
  • Barcode results come from Open Food Facts, a database anyone can edit. Photograph the label to check the pack in your hand.
  • If a result looks wrong, use "Report a wrong result" in the app so we can correct it.
